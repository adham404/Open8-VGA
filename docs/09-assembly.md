# 09 — Assembly (Rev A)

Author: Adham Elshafei

Status: **written for the Rev A board; not yet assembled.** Board: `kicad/board/open8-vga-motherboard.kicad_pcb`
(313 × 259.5 mm, 6 layers, 1.6 mm). Parts list: `hardware/fab/rev-a/open8-vga-bom-revA.csv`, generated by
`python3 hardware/bom.py`. Bring-up after assembly: [10-bring-up.md](10-bring-up.md).

## 1. What goes on the board
322 placed parts, all on the top side. The BOM groups them into 66 lines and adds 8 lines for IC
sockets and jumper shunts. `hardware/bom.py` checks every reference, footprint and value
against the routed board before it writes the CSV, and stops if one differs (Rev A: 322 of 322
match).

| Group | Qty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Logic and memory ICs | 132 | 101 × 74HC, 19 × 74AC, 9 × flash, 3 × SRAM |
| Other ICs | 3 | U10 NE555 and U90 25LC512 (DIP-8), U7 MCP130 (TO-92) |
| Ceramic capacitors | 137 | 132 × 100 nF decoupling (C7–C138), C1/C2 100 nF debounce, C3 1 µF, C4 10 nF, C5 47 nF |
| Resistors | 22 + 2 SIP-9 networks | |
| Connectors | 5 | J1 PS/2, J2 VGA, J3 USB-C, J4 logic analyser, J5 expansion |
| Controls | 5 switches, 5 jumpers, 1 trimmer | |
| Surface mount | 4 | F1, F2 (1812 polyfuses), D2 (SMB TVS), J3 (USB-C, SMD pads plus plated shell tabs) |

Everything is through-hole except those four, which are large enough to hand-solder.

**74AC vs 74HC.** 19 positions must be 74AC (BOM column `family`): U1, U2, U5, U16, U67,
U109, U114–U116, U118, U120–U122, U125, U127–U131. They carry the 25 MHz dot clock, the CPU clock
gate, the memory region decoder (read margin 13 ns with an HC138, about 41 ns with the AC138)
and the shared VRAM/font read path. See [07-parts-timing.md](07-parts-timing.md) §3–4.
**Never fit an HC part in an AC position.** The HC positions will run with AC parts, but AC has
faster edges and more ground bounce, so keep to the BOM.

**Sockets.** The nine flash chips go in 32-pin, 600 mil sockets (required: they are programmed
off-board). The BOM also lists sockets for every other DIP (recommended). The staged bring-up
in doc 10 needs them, and so does swapping a suspect chip on a 6-layer board, where
desoldering a DIP is slow. Turned-pin sockets fit the footprints: narrow DIPs sit on an
11.5 mm pitch with about 1.7 mm between courtyards. A ZIF socket's lever needs more room than
the 32-pin courtyard gives, so check in KiCad before using one.

## 2. Before soldering
1. **Bare-board check.** Measure resistance between VCC and GND (J5 pin 33 to pin 35). It must be
   open (over 1 MΩ). A low reading on a bare board is a fabrication fault: reject the board.
   Also check that VBUS and VCC are not joined (J3 VBUS pad to J5 pin 33, open before F2 is fitted).
2. **Orientation.** Every DIP footprint has rotation 0, so all 132 ICs and their sockets face the
   same way: **pin 1 top-left, notch toward the top edge** (the edge with the wide memory and
   flash rows). Exceptions: RN1, RN2 and X1 are turned 90°. The connectors are on the left edge.
3. **Labels.** Each reference is printed in the middle of its footprint, so a socket covers it.
   Print the top view (`hardware/fab/rev-a/open8-vga-top-revA.png`, or KiCad's F.Silkscreen + F.Fab at 1:1), or write
   each reference on its socket with a fine marker as you fit it. JLCPCB clips silkscreen where
   it touches pads, so a few labels near pads come out partly missing (77 DRC warnings).
4. **Heat.** The board has solid GND (In1) and VCC (In4) planes. Pins on those planes need
   more heat: about 350–370 °C, a chisel tip, and a second or two longer. The VGA connector's
   GND pins (J2 pins 5–8, 10 and the shell) connect to the plane solid, with no thermal relief.
   They need the most heat.

## 3. Soldering order
Lowest parts first, so the board lies flat on the bench for each group. Check each group
against the BOM before moving on.

| Step | Parts | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SMD:** F2 (2 A), F1 (200 mA), D2 (SMBJ5.0A), J3 (USB-C) | D2 band (cathode) to VCC. Do J3's SMD pads first, then its four shell tabs. These four are easiest while nothing else is on the board. |
| 2 | **Axial resistors** (22) | 100 Ω: R17 R18 · 470 Ω: R12 R14 R16 · 1 k: R4 R7 R21 R22 · 4.7 k: R8 R9 · 5.1 k: R19 R20 · 10 k: R1 R5 R6 R11 R13 R15 · 47 k: R2 R3 R10 |
| 3 | **D1** 1N4148 | band (cathode) to the KCLK side, as on the silkscreen |
| 4 | **RN1, RN2** 10 k SIP-9 | pin 1 (dot) = common = VCC, on the square pad |
| 5 | **Ceramic capacitors** (137) | 100 nF: C1, C2, C7–C138 · 1 µF: C3 · 10 nF: C4 · 47 nF: C5. Each decoupling cap sits just above its chip. |
| 6 | **IC sockets** (134 incl. U10, U90) | notch up. Tack two opposite corner pins, check that the socket sits flat, then solder the rest. Do the 600 mil rows (flash, SRAM, 74HC154) first. |
| 7 | **TO-92:** Q1 2N3904, U7 MCP130 | flat face as on the silkscreen; pad 1 = E (Q1) / RST (U7) |
| 8 | **LEDs** D3 (PWR), D4 (HALT) | pad 1 = cathode (short lead, flat on the rim) |
| 9 | **X1** 25.175 MHz oscillator | pin 1 = the sharp corner of the can. Solder it directly, or use a 14-pin socket (only the four corner pins are used). |
| 10 | **Pin headers** JP1–JP5 (1×2), J4, J5 (2×20) | |
| 11 | **Controls:** SW1 RESET, SW4 STEP, SW5 CONTINUE (6 mm tactile), SW2 RUN/STEP, SW3 STEP/SLOW (slide), RV1 1 M trimmer | |
| 12 | **C6** 470 µF, **BZ1** piezo | C6: mind polarity. BZ1 pin 1 (+) to VCC. |
| 13 | **Connectors:** J2 VGA (DE-15 HD), J1 PS/2 (mini-DIN) | tallest; mechanical tabs last. |

Then wash off the flux, inspect every joint (bridges between adjacent socket pins are the most
likely fault on 3,501 holes), and run **stage 1 of [10-bring-up.md](10-bring-up.md)** before you
insert any IC.

## 4. ROM images: which chip goes in which socket
All images are in `build/rom/` and built by `build.sh` (`tools/microcode.py`, `tools/alu.py`,
`tools/video.py`, `tools/build_firmware.py`). Program them off-board with any programmer that
supports the SST39SF0x0A (for example a TL866II+/T48). **Label each chip as soon as it is
programmed.** All nine look the same, and two swapped microcode chips give a CPU that fetches
correctly and then does nonsense.

| Socket | Chip | Image | Size | design.py note | What it holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U17 | SST39SF010A | `ucode0.bin` | 128 KiB | microcode ROM 0 | MC0–7: bus source (MC0–3 → U23 74HC154) and bus destination (MC4–7 → U24) |
| U18 | SST39SF010A | `ucode1.bin` | 128 KiB | microcode ROM 1 | MC8–15: ALU op (MC8–11), address select (MC12–13), PC_INC (14), PC_LOAD (15) |
| U19 | SST39SF010A | `ucode2.bin` | 128 KiB | microcode ROM 2 | MC16–23: X_INC, FLAGS_ALU, END, HALT, PC_CLR, TRAP, 2 spare |
| U38 | SST39SF040 | `alu_r0.bin` | 512 KiB | ALU ROM, ops result 0-7 | result byte for ALU ops 0–7 (drives the data bus) |
| U39 | SST39SF040 | `alu_r1.bin` | 512 KiB | ALU ROM, ops result 8-15 | result byte for ALU ops 8–15 |
| U40 | SST39SF040 | `alu_f0.bin` | 512 KiB | ALU ROM, ops flags 0-7 | flags (DQ0–3 = C Z N V) for ops 0–7, to the flag mux U28/U29 |
| U41 | SST39SF040 | `alu_f1.bin` | 512 KiB | ALU ROM, ops flags 8-15 | flags for ops 8–15 |
| U108 | SST39SF010A | `vga_timing.bin` | 128 KiB | VGA timing ROM | DQ0–5 = HSYNC#, VSYNC#, BLANK, HRESET, VRESET, VBL_START (registered by U109) |
| U200 | SST39SF010A | `firmware.bin` | 128 KiB | boot firmware (`REF_ROM`) | boot code, editor, assembler; banked pages incl. the font |

Address wiring, so a programmer dump can be checked by hand:
* **Microcode:** A0–3 = flags C Z N V, A4–7 = microstep, A8–15 = IR, **A16 = MCBANK (JP2)**,
  A17–18 tied low. The images hold the same 64 K table twice (lower half bank 0, upper half bank 1).
* **ALU:** A0–2 = op bits MC8–10, A3–10 = operand T, A11–18 = operand L. MC11 picks the ROM
  pair: U38/U40 for ops 0–7, U39/U41 for ops 8–15. The result ROMs' /OE is gated by the bus
  source decode, and the flag ROMs are always enabled.
* **Timing ROM:** A0–6 = H counter (0–99 characters), A7–16 = V counter (0–524 lines), A17–18 low.
* **Firmware:** A0–11 = CPU A0–11, A12–16 from the bank mux U70/U71 (fixed pages 0–3 at
  `$0000–$3FFF`, banked page from `$5003` at `$4000–$4FFF`), A17–18 low.

**Chip substitutes.** Every 32-pin socket is wired for the SST39SF040 pinout, with unused
high address lines tied low. A 128 KiB image therefore also works in an SST39SF020A or 040:
program it at offset 0 and leave the rest erased. The four ALU sockets need the full
512 KiB, so they must be SST39SF040.

**`font.bin` is not a chip.** The font is bank page 4 of `firmware.bin` (offset `$4000`,
identical to `build/rom/font.bin`), and the firmware copies it into the font RAM U203 at boot.
U201–U203 are SRAMs and need no programming.

**Checksums** of the images as built on 2026-10-19. A programmer usually shows a byte sum.
If you rebuild the images, re-run the command under the table.

## 5. Jumpers and switches
| Ref | Name | Open (no shunt) | Fitted | Default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| JP1 | HALF | CPU at 3.147 MHz (R5 pulls HALF_EN low) | CPU at 1.57 MHz: every other character clock | **open** |
| JP2 | MCBANK | microcode bank 0 (R6 pulls MCBANK low) | bank 1 (microcode A16) | **open** |
| JP3 | COL_R | red off (R11 pull-down) | red on | **fitted** |
| JP4 | COL_G | green off (R13) | green on | **fitted** |
| JP5 | COL_B | blue off (R15) | blue on | **fitted** |

* **JP1** is the timing fallback from [07-parts-timing.md](07-parts-timing.md) §3: half speed
  doubles every CPU margin. Fit it if the CPU misbehaves at full speed, or if an HC138 was fitted
  at U67 in place of the AC138. Video timing is not affected.
* **JP2:** the current images hold the same table in both banks, so either setting works. Bank 1
  is for trying new microcode without losing the known-good table.
* **JP3–JP5** choose the colour of the "on" pixels: all three = white, JP4 alone = green,
  JP3 + JP4 = amber/yellow. **With no colour jumper fitted the picture is black**, though the
  syncs are still there.
* **SW2 RUN/STEP:** STEP_MODE is high (single step) with the slider toward pin 1 (VCC); toward
  pin 3 it is low (RUN). **SW3 STEP/SLOW:** toward pin 1 (SLOW_SEL high), the 555 clocks the
  CPU (about 0.7 Hz with RV1 at full resistance, faster as RV1 is turned down); toward pin 3,
  each press of SW4 STEP gives one cycle. SW3 matters only in step mode. Check which way your
  switches read with a meter in stage 4 of doc 10.
* **SW1 RESET** restarts the machine (the editor text survives). **SW5 CONTINUE** releases a
  `HLT`.
